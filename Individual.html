Individual & Market Demand: Horizontal Summation

Each consumer has their own demand curve. The market demand curve is found by adding quantities demanded at each price — a horizontal sum. When consumers have different choke prices the market curve has kinks.

Market demand is the horizontal sum of individual demands: at every price, add up how much each consumer wants to buy. The market curve has kinks where consumers' choke prices differ — at high prices only low-choke-price consumers participate.
Individual demands: Qi(P) = aᵢ·max(0, P̄ᵢ − P). Consumer choke prices: P̄A = 100·aA, P̄B = 80·aB, P̄C = 60·aC. Market D = N·ΣQᵢ.

Movement Along vs. Shift of the Demand Curve

A change in the good's own price moves you along the demand curve. A change in income, tastes, or related-good prices shifts the entire curve to a new position D₁.

Income & Substitution Effects

When the price of X falls, two things happen: X is relatively cheaper (substitution effect) and the consumer's real purchasing power rises (income effect). Their sum is the total effect.
