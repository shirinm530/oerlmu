Market equilibrium: supply and demand

Surplus, Taxes, Price Controls & Quotas

Explore how markets create value — and what happens when policy gets in the way. Switch modes below: analyze a per-unit tax, drag the market price away from equilibrium, or impose a quantity cap and watch deadweight loss appear as legal trades are blocked.

Try it: In Tax Analysis mode, drag Per-unit tax (t) up to 30 — watch the red deadweight loss triangle open up between Qt and Q*. Now drag Supply steepness down toward 0.4 (inelastic supply) and check the Elasticity & Tax Incidence panel below the graph: the burden bar shifts toward "Sellers" because inelastic supply can't dodge the tax.
Try it: Switch to Market Price mode and drag the price slider above equilibrium — watch the amber excess supply band appear between Qd and Qs. Then drag it below equilibrium to see a teal excess demand band instead, and hit Move to Equilibrium to snap the price back and clear the market.
Try it: Switch to Quantity Restriction mode and pull the cap Q̄ below Q* — notice the same deadweight loss triangle reappear, this time carved out by a quota instead of a tax, as the market price rises above equilibrium.

Elasticity & Tax Incidence

📉 Demand is more elastic — buyers can easily switch away or delay purchases, so they escape most of the tax.
📈 Demand is more inelastic — buyers have few alternatives, so they absorb more of the tax.
📉 Supply is more elastic — sellers can easily adjust output, so they escape most of the tax.
📈 Supply is more inelastic — sellers can't easily adjust, so they absorb more of the tax.
